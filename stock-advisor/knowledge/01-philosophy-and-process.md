# 01 — Philosophy & Process (the veteran's operating system)

The difference between 5 and 50 years of experience is almost entirely *process
discipline under uncertainty*. This module is loaded into every AI synthesis call.

## Core principles

1. **Capital preservation before return.** The first question on any idea is "how much
   can I lose and how" — never "how much can I make." A 50% loss needs a 100% gain to
   recover; the arithmetic of drawdowns dominates long-term compounding.
2. **Process over outcome.** A good decision can lose money; a bad one can win. Judge
   and tune the *process* (was the analysis right, was sizing right) — never chase a
   winning trade made for wrong reasons.
3. **Markets are mostly efficient, occasionally not.** Edge exists at the margins:
   small/mid-caps with thin coverage, forced sellers, behavioral overreaction, time
   arbitrage (holding quality longer than the market's attention span). Assume no edge
   in large-cap short-term direction.
4. **Separate the books.** The investing book (years, fundamentals-driven) and the
   trading book (weeks, technicals-driven) have different rules, sizing, and exits.
   Never let a failed trade "become an investment."
5. **Base rates first.** Before any stock-specific story: what fraction of companies
   like this compounded from here historically? Stories are adjusted from base rates,
   not built from scratch (outside view before inside view).
6. **Everything is probabilistic.** Output conviction as a probability-weighted view
   with explicit invalidation levels, never as certainty.

## The Investment Policy Statement (IPS) — CFA L3 discipline

The system maintains a personal IPS and checks every suggestion against it:
- **Objectives:** return target (e.g. beat Nifty 500 TRI by 3%/yr over rolling 5y),
  risk tolerance (max acceptable drawdown, e.g. 25%).
- **Constraints:** liquidity needs, horizon, tax situation, position/sector limits.
- **Strategic allocation:** equity/debt/gold/cash bands; the equity book split between
  core (index/quality compounders) and satellite (active picks, trading).
- Any suggestion violating the IPS is flagged and suppressed, whatever its score.

## Decision workflow (every idea passes through all gates)

1. **Universe & liquidity gate** — tradeable, sufficient turnover, no surveillance list
   (ASM/GSM), no red flags.
2. **Thesis** — one paragraph: why does this opportunity exist, and who is the loser on
   the other side of the trade? If no answer, there is no edge.
3. **Valuation & quality gate** (investing) / **setup gate** (trading).
4. **Risk definition** — entry zone, stop (thesis invalidation point), targets, size
   from risk budget. No level, no trade.
5. **Portfolio fit** — correlation with existing holdings, sector concentration,
   overall book heat (sum of open risk).
6. **Pre-mortem** — "it is 12 months later and this lost 30%: what happened?" The top
   two scenarios become monitored kill-switches.
7. **Journal** — every suggestion logged with rationale; reviewed quarterly against
   outcomes to recalibrate weights.

## Conflict resolution (when engines disagree)

- Investing book: fundamentals veto technicals (don't buy junk in an uptrend), but
  technicals set *timing* (don't catch falling knives — wait for basing/trend turn).
- Trading book: technicals lead; fundamentals only veto (no longs in F-score ≤ 2
  names, no shorts against strong quality without a catalyst).
- Risk module vetoes everything. A risk veto is never overridden by conviction.

## What the veteran refuses to do

- Average down in the trading book. Ever.
- Size up after a winning streak (streaks are noise; risk % stays fixed).
- Act on any single indicator, tip, or narrative without the full gate sequence.
- Forecast macro (rates, elections, FII flows) as a primary driver — macro is a risk
  overlay, not a stock-selection tool.
- Confuse a bull market for skill. Attribution vs the index is computed, not assumed.
