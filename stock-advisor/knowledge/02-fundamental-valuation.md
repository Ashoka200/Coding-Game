# 02 — Fundamental Analysis & Valuation (CFA L2 equity, practitioner-tuned)

## Business quality before price (the checklist order matters)

1. **Economics:** ROCE and ROIC vs cost of capital, sustained >15% for quality; gross
   margin stability (pricing power proxy); asset turns × margins (DuPont: know *where*
   ROE comes from — leverage-driven ROE is low quality).
2. **Growth quality:** revenue CAGR decomposed into volume vs price vs acquisition;
   incremental ROCE (does growth create or destroy value); reinvestment runway.
3. **Balance sheet:** net debt/EBITDA < 2 (sector-adjusted), interest coverage > 4,
   working-capital cycle trend (rising receivable days = channel stuffing risk).
4. **Cash conversion:** CFO/EBITDA > 70% over 5y; FCF positive over a cycle. Profit
   without cash is an accrual, and accruals mean-revert (Sloan).
5. **Governance (critical in India):** promoter pledging (any rise = flag; >20% = veto),
   related-party transactions, auditor resignations, royalty payouts to parent,
   frequent capital raises, promoter salary vs profit.

## Earnings quality forensics (Level II FSA)

- Accruals ratio = (NI − CFO)/avg total assets; high accruals → earnings likely overstated.
- Beneish M-Score for manipulation risk; Altman Z for distress (mfg names).
- Revenue recognition red flags: receivables growing faster than sales for 2+ years,
  unbilled revenue spikes, one-off "other income" dressing up EPS.
- Capitalization games: rising capitalized costs (interest, R&D, development) without
  asset productivity.

## Valuation — always triangulate three lenses, never one number

1. **Intrinsic (DCF / owner earnings):**
   - FCFF discounted at WACC; terminal value ≤ 60% of total or the model is a guess.
   - Reverse DCF preferred: what growth does the current price imply? Judge whether
     that hurdle is plausible — this avoids false precision.
   - For financials: excess-return / residual income models (P/B vs ROE−g framework),
     never FCFF.
2. **Relative:** P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B vs (a) own 10y percentile band and (b) sector
   median. A stock at its 90th percentile valuation needs an explicit "what changed"
   answer.
3. **Yield lens:** earnings yield + growth vs 10y G-Sec; dividend/buyback yield for
   mature names.

Output is a **fair-value range** (bear/base/bull with probabilities), never a point.
Margin of safety required: 25–30% below base fair value for entry on average quality,
10–15% acceptable for exceptional quality with long runways.

## Sector-specific adjustments

- **Banks/NBFCs:** P/B vs ROE, NIM trend, GNPA/NNPA, credit cost cycle, CASA, capital
  adequacy. Book value is the earnings machine — asset quality doubts void the book.
- **IT services:** dollar revenue growth, deal TCV, attrition, margin bands, currency.
- **Pharma:** US generics pricing vs domestic branded mix, USFDA status per plant.
- **Commodities/cyclicals:** value on mid-cycle earnings and P/B, never peak P/E
  (low P/E at cycle top is a trap; high P/E at bottom is often the buy).
- **Consumer:** volume growth vs price-led growth, distribution expansion, ad-spend
  as investment.

## Scoring output (engine spec)

Composite fundamental score 0–100 = Quality 40% + Growth 25% + Valuation 25% +
Governance 10% (governance can only subtract and can hard-veto). Piotroski F-Score
computed as a cross-check; F ≤ 3 blocks the investing book regardless of composite.
