Trench Defense

You're in the trench, Commander. Enemy infantry and armor are advancing on your position in waves. Tap an enemy to fire. 8 rounds per magazine — watch your reloads. Don't let them reach the wire.

Field manual · how a 3D shooter actually works

The 3D is an illusion. There is no real 3D here — every enemy has a distance variable, and the renderer simply draws far things small and high, near things big and low. Your brain does the rest. Real engines (the ones behind Battlefield) do the same trick with mathematics called projection — just millions of times more of it, on the graphics card.

Enemies live in an ARRAY — a list variable holding many soldier OBJECTS at once. Every frame, a loop walks the whole list: move each enemy closer, draw it, check if it reached you. One loop, any number of enemies. Arrays are your Stage 2 weapon — this game is why you'll want them.

Shooting is HIT DETECTION: when you tap, the code asks every enemy "does the tap point sit inside your rectangle?" That question — do two shapes overlap? — is called collision detection, and it powers every shooter, racing game, and platformer ever made.

The game has STATES: fighting, reloading, between waves, game over — and the code behaves differently in each. That pattern is called a state machine. You already met it: Stage 1's play-again loop was a tiny one.
